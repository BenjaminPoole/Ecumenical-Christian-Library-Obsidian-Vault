# Four Letters to Dr Bentley

*Isaac Newton* — first printed in 1756; the text of Alexander Dyce's *Works of Richard Bentley* vol. 3 (1838), read again from the page images at [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/worksrichardben04newtgoog).

Newton's answers to Richard Bentley, who was preparing the first Boyle Lectures, *A Confutation of Atheism*, and wrote to ask how far the *Principia* could be used in them. They are the fullest statement Newton made of the argument from the frame of the world to its maker: that gravity alone could not have divided matter into a shining sun and dark planets, nor given the planets their transverse motions, nor set the system in its order; and that gravity itself, acting at a distance without contact, is *so great an absurdity* that it must be caused by an agent. Bentley kept them, and they were printed after his death.
