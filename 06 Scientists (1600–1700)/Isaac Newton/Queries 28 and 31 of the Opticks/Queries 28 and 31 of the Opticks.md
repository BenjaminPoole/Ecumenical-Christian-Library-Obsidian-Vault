# Queries 28 and 31 of the Opticks

*Isaac Newton* — from the fourth edition of the *Opticks* (London, 1730), at [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33504).

The two of the thirty-one questions closing the *Opticks* in which Newton follows natural philosophy to its end in God. The twenty-eighth argues against filling space with a medium and asks *whence is it that Nature doth nothing in vain*, and whether space is not *the Sensory of a Being incorporeal, living, intelligent, omnipresent*; the thirty-first, after a long survey of chemical attraction, ends with God forming matter *in solid, massy, hard, impenetrable, moveable Particles* in the beginning, and with the hope that natural philosophy perfected will enlarge moral philosophy too. Both are given whole, since the theology closes an argument that begins with light and salts.
