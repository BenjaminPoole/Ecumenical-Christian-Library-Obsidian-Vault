# Some Considerations Touching the Style of the Holy Scriptures

*Robert Boyle* — first published in 1661; the text of *Treatises on the High Veneration Man's Intellect Owes to God, on Things above Reason, and on the Style of the Holy Scriptures* (London, 1836), read again from the page images at [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/treatisesonthehi00boyluoft); the four pages missing from that scan are supplied from Birch's *Works* vol. 2 (1744).

Boyle's defence of the Bible's style, written in his twenties as part of a longer essay on the Scriptures and addressed to a friend he calls Theophilus. He answers eight objections to the Scripture's manner of writing: that it is obscure, immethodical, incoherent, full of things trivial or repeated, self-contradictory, less instructive than other books, and unadorned by rhetoric; the last answer runs into a long digression against profaneness and an appendix inviting men of wit to write for religion. The headings dividing the discourse into notes are the book's own; the note titles for the opening considerations and the first objection, which the book leaves unheaded, are the library's.
