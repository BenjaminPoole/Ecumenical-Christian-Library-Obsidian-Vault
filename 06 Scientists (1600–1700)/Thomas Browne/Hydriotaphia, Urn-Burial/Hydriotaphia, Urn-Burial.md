# Hydriotaphia, Urn-Burial

*Thomas Browne* — the text of the folio of 1686, as J. W. Willis Bund printed it in 1869, from [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/586).

Occasioned by some urns dug up in a Norfolk field, and turning, by way of the burial customs of every nation Browne had read of, into a meditation on death, oblivion and the resurrection of the body. The last chapter is the most famous prose Browne wrote: *But man is a noble animal, splendid in ashes, and pompous in the grave.* Printed with *The Garden of Cyrus*, which is not theology and is not given.
