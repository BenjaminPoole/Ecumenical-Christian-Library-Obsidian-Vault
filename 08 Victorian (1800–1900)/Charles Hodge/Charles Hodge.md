# Charles Hodge

Charles Hodge (1797–1878), professor at Princeton Theological Seminary for over fifty years and its principal for most of them, founder and editor of the *Biblical Repertory and Princeton Review*, and the leading voice of Old School Presbyterianism and of Reformed orthodoxy in nineteenth-century America. He taught some three thousand ministers, and liked to say that no new idea had ever originated at Princeton. His *Systematic Theology* was the standard American Reformed dogmatics for two generations.

## Works (7)

- **[[A Commentary on the Epistle to the Ephesians|A Commentary on the Epistle to the Ephesians]]** — An exposition of Ephesians verse by verse, each chapter opening with an analysis of its argument. (7 notes)
- **[[Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans|Commentary on the Epistle to the Romans]]** — Hodge's commentary on Romans, first published in 1835 and here rewritten, the book through which he taught Paul's doctrine of justification to two generations of Princeton students. An introduction on Paul, the church at Rome and the plan of the epistle is followed by the sixteen chapters, each taken section by section: a summary of contents, an analysis of the argument, a verse-by-verse commentary weighing the Greek text and the interpreters from the Fathers to Tholuck and De Wette, and the doctrines and practical remarks the passage teaches. (17 notes)
- **[[Princeton Sermons|Princeton Sermons]]** — The outlines of the addresses Hodge gave to the students of Princeton Theological Seminary on Sabbath afternoons, from 1852 to 1878, edited after his death by his son A. A. Hodge and arranged under ten heads: God and his attributes; Christ, his person and offices; the Holy Spirit; Satan, sin and sins; conversion; Christian experience and privileges; Christian duties; the means of grace; death and the consummation of redemption; and the papers of his last year. 249 outlines, each with the date it was given, together with A. A. Hodge's preface on the Sabbath conference at Princeton. (250 notes)
- **[[Systematic Theology|Systematic Theology]]** — The summa of Old Princeton: fifty years of teaching at Princeton Theological Seminary set out in three volumes — an Introduction on theological method and the rule of faith, then Theology Proper, Anthropology, Soteriology and Eschatology. Hodge's theologian is to be "governed by the same rules as a man of science": the Scriptures contain the facts, and theology arranges them. (314 notes)
- **[[The Church and its Polity|The Church and its Polity]]** — Hodge's writing on the doctrine and government of the Church, gathered after his death: the idea of the Church as the communion of saints against the Oxford and Roman theories, its visibility and perpetuity, Church and State, Presbyterianism and the orders of the Church of England; then the Presbyterian constitution applied article by article, from the particular church and its officers through the presbytery and General Assembly to discipline. The same book as *Discussions in Church Polity* (1878). (53 notes)
- **[[The Way of Life|The Way of Life]]** — Hodge's plain answer, written for intelligent young readers, to two questions: are the Scriptures the word of God, and what do they teach a man to believe and experience in order to be saved? Nine chapters take the reader from the evidence for Scripture through sin, indifference and conviction, to justification, faith and repentance, the profession of religion and the sacraments, and holy living. (10 notes)
- **[[What is Darwinism|What is Darwinism]]** — Hodge's answer to the question in his title: not evolution as such, but natural selection as Darwin meant it — the exclusion of design from nature. "What is Darwinism? It is Atheism." (8 notes)
