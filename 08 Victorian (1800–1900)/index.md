# Victorian (1800–1900)

The nineteenth century, Catholic and Protestant: the Victorian pulpit, the Oxford Movement, Old Princeton and Dutch Neo-Calvinism, and the German and Danish theology that answered the Enlightenment. All of it is in the public domain, with one exception noted under Kierkegaard.

## Friedrich Schleiermacher

Friedrich Schleiermacher (1768–1834), preacher in Berlin and professor at Halle and Berlin, called the father of modern liberal theology: religion is neither knowing nor doing but feeling, "the feeling of absolute dependence". *On Religion: Speeches to Its Cultured Despisers* in John Oman's translation (1893), and the *Selected Sermons* translated by Mary F. Wilson (1890), from the [CCEL](https://ccel.org/ccel/schleiermach).

Author index: **[[Friedrich Schleiermacher|Friedrich Schleiermacher]]** *(35 notes)*

## Charles Hodge

Charles Hodge (1797–1878), for over fifty years professor at Princeton Theological Seminary and the leading voice of Old School Presbyterianism in America. The three-volume *Systematic Theology* (1871–73), the *Commentary on Ephesians*, and *What is Darwinism?* (1874), from the [CCEL](https://ccel.org/ccel/hodge).

Author index: **[[Charles Hodge|Charles Hodge]]** *(329 notes)*

## John Henry Newman

John Henry Newman (1801–1890), the leader of the Oxford Movement, received into the Catholic Church in 1845, cardinal in 1879, and canonised in 2019. His works are here in the uniform edition: the *Parochial and Plain Sermons* and the *Oxford University Sermons*, *The Arians of the Fourth Century*, the *Lectures on Justification*, the *Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine*, *The Idea of a University*, the *Apologia pro Vita Sua* (both texts), the *Grammar of Assent*, the two novels, *The Dream of Gerontius* and the rest of the verse, and the essays, sketches, tracts and controversial writings. Text from the [Newman Reader](https://www.newmanreader.org/works/).

Author index: **[[John Henry Newman|John Henry Newman]]** *(1,038 notes)*

## Søren Kierkegaard

Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855), the Danish philosopher and theologian of the single individual before God, and the forerunner of Christian existentialism. Lee M. Hollander's *Selections from the Writings of Kierkegaard* (1923), the first Kierkegaard in English; *The Crowd is Untruth* in a modern translation that its translator, Charles K. Bellinger, has placed in the public domain; and *Works of Love* in David F. and Lillian Marvin Swenson's translation (Princeton, 1946), whose copyright was never renewed and which is therefore public domain in the United States — it may still be in copyright elsewhere. The other English translations (Swenson, Lowrie, the Hongs) date from 1936 onward and were renewed, or were first published in Britain, and are left out.

Author index: **[[Søren Kierkegaard|Søren Kierkegaard]]** *(30 notes)*

## J. C. Ryle

John Charles Ryle (1816–1900), first Bishop of Liverpool and the outstanding evangelical Anglican writer of the Victorian era. He spent thirty-eight years in country parishes in Suffolk before Disraeli sent him to Liverpool at sixty-four, and he wrote as he preached — plainly, directly, and for ordinary people. *Holiness*, the *Expository Thoughts on the Gospels*, *Practical Religion*, *Christian Leaders of the Last Century*, *Coming Events and Present Duties*, *The Upper Room*, and the shorter tracts.

Author index: **[[JC Ryle|J. C. Ryle]]** *(214 notes)*

## Charles Spurgeon

Charles Haddon Spurgeon (1834–1892), the "Prince of Preachers," was the Reformed Baptist pastor of the New Park Street Chapel and then the Metropolitan Tabernacle in London for thirty-eight years. He left one of the largest bodies of published work of any Christian author: the weekly sermons alone run to 63 volumes and 3,512 sermons, preached between 1855 and 1917. Alongside them are *The Treasury of David*, *Morning and Evening*, *Lectures to My Students*, *All of Grace*, *The Soul Winner*, *Faith's Checkbook*, *John Ploughman's Talk*, *Commenting and Commentaries*, *Till He Come*, the *Sermons on Proverbs*, and *A Puritan Catechism*. Texts from the [Christian Classics Ethereal Library](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon) and [The Spurgeon Archive](https://archive.spurgeon.org/treasury/treasury.php).

Author index: **[[Charles Spurgeon|Charles Spurgeon]]** *(4,633 notes)*

## Abraham Kuyper

Abraham Kuyper (1837–1920), founder of the Free University of Amsterdam and of Neo-Calvinism, and Prime Minister of the Netherlands from 1901 to 1905. The Stone Lectures on *Calvinism* given at Princeton in 1898, read from the page images of the 1899 edition; *The Work of the Holy Spirit* (De Vries, 1900, with an introduction by B. B. Warfield) and the meditations *To Be Near Unto God* (1918), from the [CCEL](https://ccel.org/ccel/kuyper).

Author index: **[[Abraham Kuyper|Abraham Kuyper]]** *(242 notes)*

## Benjamin B. Warfield

Benjamin Breckinridge Warfield (1851–1921), professor of theology at Princeton Theological Seminary from 1887 until his death, the successor of the Hodges and the last great theologian of Old Princeton. The sermons of *The Power of God unto Salvation* (1903) and *The Saviour of the World* (1913), *The Lord of Glory* (1907), his study of the New Testament's designations of Christ, *The Plan of Salvation* (1915) and *Counterfeit Miracles* (1918), read from the page images of the first editions.

Author index: **[[Benjamin B. Warfield|Benjamin B. Warfield]]** *(49 notes)*
