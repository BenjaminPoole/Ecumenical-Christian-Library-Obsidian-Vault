# John Henry Newman

**John Henry Newman (1801–1890)** was born in London, brought up an evangelical Anglican, and
underwent at fifteen the conversion he describes in the *Apologia*. He became a Fellow of Oriel
in 1822 and Vicar of St Mary the Virgin, Oxford, in 1828, and from 1833 led the Oxford Movement
with Keble and Pusey, writing a large share of the *Tracts for the Times*. In 1845 he was
received into the Catholic Church. He founded the Birmingham Oratory in 1848, was the first
rector of the Catholic University of Ireland (1854–1858), and was made a cardinal by Leo XIII
in 1879. He was beatified in 2010, canonised in 2019, and declared a Doctor of the Church in 2025.

Newman's Anglican sermons and his *Grammar of Assent* are read by Protestants and Catholics
alike. The *Essay on Development*, the *Apologia* and the *Letter to the Duke of Norfolk* are
the works that explain why he left the Church of England, and they are the ones Protestant
readers most often argue with.

**1,038 notes.** Newman's own works are all here, with the exceptions listed below, most
of them in the uniform edition (Longmans, Green & Co.) that he revised himself.

## Sources and honesty about them

- **Text.** Every note is taken from the [Newman Reader](https://www.newmanreader.org/works/)
  of the National Institute for Newman Studies. Its transcriptions follow the printed volumes
  page by page and are the only complete digital Newman. The site claims copyright in its
  presentation (2007). Newman's text is public domain, and it is the only thing taken.
- **Left out of every note:** the Newman Reader's navigation, its inline page numbers `{123}`,
  its editorial insertions marked `—NR`, its "Background" and "Links" notes, the reviews it
  prints beside some works, and Hilaire Belloc's 1931 foreword to the *Apologia*. Newman's own
  footnotes are kept, as footnotes.
- **The *Apologia* is here twice, deliberately.** *Apologia pro Vita Sua* is the 1865 revision
  that Newman kept in print. *Apologia pro Vita Sua (1864)* is Wilfrid Ward's 1913 Oxford
  edition of the original pamphlets, which carries the Kingsley correspondence and marks every
  difference between the two texts, with double square brackets round words of 1864 dropped in
  1865 and angle brackets round words added. Its Reading Note explains the marks.
- **Lost in transcription:** a few Hebrew words in the *British Critic* notices, which the
  Newman Reader gives only as images.
- **Not included:**
  - *Faith and Prejudice and Other Unpublished Sermons*, first published in 1956, which may
    still be in copyright as a posthumous first edition;
  - the *Letters and Diaries*, a modern critical edition (1961–2008) that is in copyright;
  - *Sayings of Cardinal Newman*, a compilation by another hand;
  - Newman's translations of others: Athanasius's *Select Treatises* (the vault has NPNF's),
    Bishop Andrewes's Greek devotions (Tract 88), and the Fleury and *Catena Aurea* volumes he
    edited;
  - the Latin *Hymni Ecclesiae*, which Newman edited rather than wrote.
