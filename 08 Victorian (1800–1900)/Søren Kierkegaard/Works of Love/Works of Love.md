# Works of Love

*Søren Kierkegaard* — *Works of Love: Some Christian Reflections in the Form of Discourses*, translated from the Danish by David F. Swenson and Lillian Marvin Swenson, with an introduction by Douglas V. Steere (Princeton University Press, 1946; second printing 1949), read again from the page images of the [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.187384). **Copyright:** the 1946 copyright was not renewed (the Stanford Copyright Renewal Database holds renewals only for the Hongs' separate translation of 1962), so this translation is in the public domain in the United States; Lillian Swenson's dates make it likely still in copyright elsewhere. The translators' notes at the end are transcribed from the page images and keyed, as in the book, to its page numbers.

Kierkegaard's greatest work of Christian ethics, published under his own name in 1847: eighteen discourses in two series on the commandment to love the neighbour — that love is a duty, that it is known by its fruits, that it is the fulfilment of the law, that it believes, hopes, seeks not its own, covers a multitude of sins and abides — against the preferential love of the poet and the lover.
