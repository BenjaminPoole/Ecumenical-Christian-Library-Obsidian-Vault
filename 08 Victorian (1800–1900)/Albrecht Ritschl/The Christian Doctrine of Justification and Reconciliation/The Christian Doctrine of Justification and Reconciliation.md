# The Christian Doctrine of Justification and Reconciliation

*Albrecht Ritschl* — *The Christian Doctrine of Justification and Reconciliation: The Positive Development of the Doctrine*, English translation edited by H. R. Mackintosh and A. B. Macaulay (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1900), transcribed from the page images of the [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/thechristiandoct00ritsuoft).

The third and constructive volume of Ritschl's *Die christliche Lehre von der Rechtfertigung und Versöhnung* (1870–1874), translated from the third German edition (1888). After an introduction on the place of the doctrine in theology and on the theory of knowledge it assumes, Ritschl defines justification as the forgiveness of sins by which God admits sinners into fellowship with Himself; sets out its relation to the Christian community and the Kingdom of God; treats in turn the doctrines of God, of sin, and of Christ's person and life-work; argues for the necessity of forgiveness and for basing it on the work and suffering of Christ; and closes with the religious functions — faith in providence, humility, patience, prayer — that spring from reconciliation, and with the moral life of the Christian in his calling. The index at the end of the book is left out.
