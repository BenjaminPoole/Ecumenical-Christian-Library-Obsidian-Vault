# Albrecht Ritschl

Albrecht Ritschl (1822–1889), professor at Bonn and from 1864 at Göttingen, and after Schleiermacher the most influential German Protestant theologian of the century. Having begun in the Tübingen school of F. C. Baur, he broke with it over the origins of the early Church, and went on to build a theology on the historical revelation in Christ and the community's experience of it, setting metaphysics aside: religious statements are value-judgments, and the Kingdom of God, the ethical fellowship of mankind, is the goal of the Christian life. His three-volume *Justification and Reconciliation* (1870–74) founded the Ritschlian school of Harnack and Herrmann.

## Works (1)

- **[[The Christian Doctrine of Justification and Reconciliation|The Christian Doctrine of Justification and Reconciliation]]** — The third and constructive volume of Ritschl's *Die christliche Lehre von der Rechtfertigung und Versöhnung* (1870–1874), translated from the third German edition (1888). After an introduction on the place of the doctrine in theology and on the theory of knowledge it assumes, Ritschl defines justification as the forgiveness of sins by which God admits sinners into fellowship with Himself; sets out its relation to the Christian community and the Kingdom of God; treats in turn the doctrines of God, of sin, and of Christ's person and life-work; argues for the necessity of forgiveness and for basing it on the work and suffering of Christ; and closes with the religious functions — faith in providence, humility, patience, prayer — that spring from reconciliation, and with the moral life of the Christian in his calling. The index at the end of the book is left out. (12 notes)
