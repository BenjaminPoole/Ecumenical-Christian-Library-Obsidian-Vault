# Predestination in the Reformed Confessions

*Benjamin B. Warfield* — *Predestination in the Reformed Confessions* (from *The Presbyterian and Reformed Review*, xii. 1901, pp. 49-128), from the [Monergism](https://www.monergism.com/thethreshold/sdg/warfield/warfield_predestconf.html) text, marked up by Lance George Marshall. It has not been read in full against the print; see "Works taken from an e-text" in `docs/transcription.md`. Marshall's bracketed cross-references to the Westminster Confession ("[WC III. v.]") are left out; the brackets that remain are Warfield's. The [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/predestinationin00warf) is the print.

Warfield surveys what the Reformed confessions of 1523-1675 say of predestination. The first part follows the documents from Zwingli and the Swiss through Calvin and his pupils to the German, Brandenburg and Thorn confessions, noting how cautiously some of them treat reprobation and election. The second sets out the confessions themselves, in their own words, from the Zwinglian and Bohemian to the Genevan, French, Belgic, Scottish, English and Irish articles, the Westminster standards, the Canons of Dort and the Helvetic Consensus, with the points they hold in common: the sovereignty of election, the reality of reprobation, the unity of the decree and the infralapsarian or supralapsarian view of its object.
