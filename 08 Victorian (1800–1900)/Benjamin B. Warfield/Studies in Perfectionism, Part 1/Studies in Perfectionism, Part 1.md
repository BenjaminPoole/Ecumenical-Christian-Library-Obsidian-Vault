# Studies in Perfectionism, Part 1

*Benjamin B. Warfield* — *Studies in Perfectionism*, part 1, volume VII of his works (New York: Oxford University Press, 1931), from the [Monergism](https://www.monergism.com/thethreshold/sdg/warfield/Vol%207_Studies%20in%20Perfectionism%20Part%201_Warfield.pdf) edition (2026; the EPUB, which has no line-end hyphens and keeps the footnote figures), whose text was scanned, proofread and marked up by Lance George Marshall. Monergism's title page, and the descriptive sub-titles it adds to the articles ("Ritschle the Rationalist" and the like), are left out. Every footnote is set as a footnote. It has not been read against the print; see "Works taken from an e-text" in `docs/transcription.md`. The [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/perfectionism0001warf_g3y8) is the print. Published in 1931, it is in the public domain in the United States (the user confirmed this for the volumes of 1931-32); the committee's prefatory note and the other editorial matter of the volume may still be in copyright elsewhere.

The German half of Warfield's study of perfectionism, the seventh volume of his works: *Albrecht Ritschl and His Doctrine of Christian Perfection* (two articles), "*Miserable-Sinner Christianity*" *in the Hands of the Rationalists* (three articles, from Ritschl and Wernle to Pfleiderer and Windisch), "*Die Heiligungsbewegung*" (the German Fellowship Movement and its sanctification teaching, from Pearsall Smith's campaign to the Gnadau Conference), and *The German Higher Life Movement in Its Chief Exponent*, Theodor Jellinghaus. Warfield asks of each what the perfectionism of the day taught and how far the claim to sinlessness agrees with the Reformed doctrine of sin and grace. Most of the articles first appeared in *The Princeton Theological Review* (1919-20); *Die Heiligungsbewegung* in *Bibliotheca Sacra* and the study of Jellinghaus in *The Biblical Review* (1919).
