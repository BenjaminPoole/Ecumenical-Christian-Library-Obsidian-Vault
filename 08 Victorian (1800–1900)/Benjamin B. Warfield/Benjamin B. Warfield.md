# Benjamin B. Warfield

Benjamin Breckinridge Warfield (1851–1921), professor of didactic and polemic theology at Princeton Theological Seminary from 1887 until his death, the successor of Charles and A. A. Hodge and the last great theologian of Old Princeton. A scholar of the New Testament text before he was a dogmatician, he defended the inspiration and inerrancy of Scripture, the Westminster Standards and the Augustinian doctrine of grace against the liberalism and perfectionism of his day, in some thirty books and a great many articles and reviews.

## Works (16)

- **[[Biblical Doctrines|Biblical Doctrines]]** — Sixteen articles on the doctrines of the Bible, mostly written for Hastings's dictionaries and the *International Standard Bible Encyclopaedia*: predestination, the foresight of Jesus, the Spirit of God in the Old Testament, the biblical doctrine of the Trinity, the person of Christ, "God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ", the Christ that Paul preached, Jesus' own account of His mission, the New Testament terms for redemption, Christ our sacrifice, renewal, faith, the terminology of love, the prophecies of St. Paul, and the millennium and the Apocalypse. (18 notes)
- **[[Calvin as a Theologian|Calvin as a Theologian]]** — An address on what manner of theologian Calvin was. Warfield asks the hearer to put aside the picture of Calvin as a speculative and logical audacity and shows instead his positiveness, his dependence on Scripture and on the Spirit's witness, and his place as the systematiser of the Reformation, the theologian of the Holy Spirit. (1 notes)
- **[[Calvin's Doctrine of God|Calvin's Doctrine of God]]** — Warfield gathers Calvin's doctrine of God from the *Institutes*, which have no formal chapter on it: the sense of deity and the knowledge of God, God's nature and attributes, His majesty and fatherhood, the jealousy for His honour that Calvin's war on idolatry shows, with the incidental matters of images, the pictorial arts and preaching, and Calvin's doctrine of the Trinity. Calvin, he argues, is no cold logician but a man whose theology is religion, a reverent sobriety before the God he knows. (1 notes)
- **[[Christology and Criticism|Christology and Criticism]]** — Warfield's historico-critical articles on the person and work of Christ: the divine Messiah in the Old Testament, the people's misconception of Jesus and the charge of blasphemy, Jesus' alleged confession of sin, the article on Jesus Christ, Schmiedel's "pillar-passages", the "two natures" and recent christological speculation, "Christless Christianity" (against Drews), the twentieth-century Christ, the essence of Christianity and the cross, and an appendix on the supernatural birth of Jesus. (12 notes)
- **[[Counterfeit Miracles|Counterfeit Miracles]]** — The Thomas Smyth Lectures for 1917–1918, delivered at Columbia Theological Seminary, South Carolina. Warfield argues that the miraculous gifts of the apostolic age were the credentials of the apostles and ceased with them, and then examines the claims to miracle that followed: the marvels of the Fathers and the Middle Ages, the miracles of Lourdes and the Roman Church, the tongues and prophecies of Edward Irving's congregation, the faith-healing movement, and the mind-cure of Christian Science. (12 notes)
- **[[The Development of the Doctrine of Infant Salvation|The Development of the Doctrine of Infant Salvation]]** — Warfield traces how the doctrine of the salvation of infants dying in infancy has been held in every age of the Church and yet corrected: the Fathers' necessity of baptism and Augustine's mildest damnation, the medieval mitigation, the teaching of Rome, the Lutheran, Anglican and Reformed doctrines from Zwingli to Dort and Westminster, the "ethical" tendencies of later Protestantism, and the doctrinal development which makes the salvation of the infant rest on God's electing grace. (1 notes)
- **[[Faith and Life|Faith and Life]]** — Forty-one addresses given to the students at the Sunday afternoon conferences in the seminary's Oratory, each on a text of Scripture: Elijah at Horeb and Old Testament religion, the wrath of man, childlikeness, the conviction and the outpouring of the Spirit, prayer, the witness of the Spirit to our sonship, the alien righteousness, peace with God, entire sanctification, the mystery of godliness, and God's holiness and ours. (41 notes)
- **[[The Gospel of the Incarnation|The Gospel of the Incarnation]]** — Two sermons on the incarnation. The first, on John 6:38-39, takes "the end of the incarnation": Christ came down from heaven to do the Father's will, which is the salvation of all that the Father has given Him, and the sermon draws out what this says of the covenant of redemption, the particularity of salvation, and the believer's assurance. The second, on Philippians 2:5-8, takes the example of the incarnation: the self-emptying of Christ as the pattern of the Christian's self-sacrifice. (2 notes)
- **[[The Lord of Glory|The Lord of Glory]]** — Warfield surveys every title the New Testament gives Jesus — Jesus, Christ, Lord, Son of Man, Son of God — book by book, from Mark through the Apocalypse, to show that the conviction of His deity is not a late layer laid over the gospel story but the presupposition of every part of the New Testament, the Synoptic Gospels and their supposed primitive sources included. (14 notes)
- **[[The Making of the Westminster Confession|The Making of the Westminster Confession]]** — Warfield's study of the Westminster Assembly's making of the Confession from its Minutes. The first part sets out how the Confession was made: the commission of the Solemn League and Covenant, the Committees and the order of their reports, the chapters one by one with the dates of their debate, and what the Assembly sent to Parliament. The second takes the third chapter, *Of God's Eternal Decree*, and follows its formulation against the Irish Articles of 1615, with the three versions set in parallel columns and the debates on each section, especially the sixth. (2 notes)
- **[[The Plan of Salvation|The Plan of Salvation]]** — Warfield sets out the possible answers to the question how God saves, as a series of choices, each narrowing the last: naturalism or supernaturalism (autosoterism against salvation from God), sacerdotalism or evangelicalism (the Church as the channel of grace, or God working immediately on the soul), universalism or particularism, and, within particularism, the order of the decrees — ending in Calvinism as evangelicalism consistently held. (6 notes)
- **[[The Power of God unto Salvation|The Power of God unto Salvation]]** — Eight sermons preached in the chapel of the Theological Seminary at Princeton: on the revelation of man in Christ, the saving Christ, the argument from Christian experience, the paradox of omnipotence, the love of the Holy Ghost, the leading of the Spirit, Paul's earliest gospel in 1 Thessalonians, and false religion and the true. (8 notes)
- **[[Revelation and Inspiration|Revelation and Inspiration]]** — Ten essays and two appendices on the Bible as revelation and as the inspired word of God: the biblical idea and the theories of revelation, the biblical idea of inspiration, the New Testament's use of "Scripture", "God-inspired" (*theopneustos*), "It says" and "Scripture says" as one, "the oracles of God", the real problem of inspiration and its relation to criticism, and, in the appendices, the divine origin of the Bible and the formation of the New Testament canon. The first volume of the works published under the will of Warfield's committee. (14 notes)
- **[[The Right of Systematic Theology|The Right of Systematic Theology]]** — The introduction by James Orr, and Warfield's article on the right of systematic theology to exist, first printed in *The Presbyterian and Reformed Review*. Against the claims that Christianity is life and not doctrine, fact and not theory, feeling and not thought, and that doctrine is only a passing form, Warfield argues from Christianity's own character: it comes as truth, makes its appeal to the mind, and so asks to be set in order. He draws on the French and German writers of the day, Sabatier, Astié, Bois, Rivier, Ritschl and others. (2 notes)
- **[[The Saviour of the World|The Saviour of the World]]** — Nine sermons preached to the students of Princeton Seminary, each opening with its text: the prodigal son, the one Name under heaven, the Lamb of God, God's immeasurable love in John 3:16, the gospel of Paul, the glorified Christ, the risen Jesus, the gospel of the covenant, and the imitation of the incarnation in Philippians 2. (9 notes)
- **[[The Westminster Assembly and Its Work|The Westminster Assembly and Its Work]]** — Two articles from *The Princeton Theological Review* of 1908. The first is the history of the calling of the Westminster Assembly: the conflict of King and Parliament over the Church, the Long Parliament, the ordinance of 1643 that convened the Divines to advise it, and the Solemn League and Covenant with the Scots that turned an advisory body into the framer of a uniform religion for three kingdoms. The second follows its work: the Directory for Church Government and for Ordination, the Directory for Worship, the Confession of Faith, the Larger and Shorter Catechisms, and what became of them. (2 notes)
