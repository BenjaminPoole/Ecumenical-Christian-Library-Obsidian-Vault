# Calvin's Doctrine of the Knowledge of God

*Benjamin B. Warfield* — *Calvin's Doctrine of the Knowledge of God* (from *The Princeton Theological Review*, vii. 1909, pp. 219-325; reprinted in *Calvin and the Reformation*, 1909), from the [Monergism](https://www.monergism.com/thethreshold/sdg/warfield/warfield_calvinknowledge.html) text, marked up by Lance George Marshall. It has been collated against the scan's OCR but not read in full; see "Works taken from an e-text" in `docs/transcription.md`. The essay is Warfield's contribution to *Calvin and the Reformation* (New York: Revell, 1909), a volume of four essays by different authors; the others are not in the library. The [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/calvinandreform00warfgoog) is the print.

Warfield examines the first chapters of Calvin's *Institutes* to show how Calvin grounded the knowledge of God. He begins with natural revelation, the sense of deity written on every heart and the witness of creation, and shows why sin leaves it ineffective. He then sets out Calvin's doctrine of Holy Scripture and its inspiration, and the marks by which it proves itself divine. The longest part treats the testimony of the Holy Spirit, its mode, its object and its place in the religious life, as the ground of faith's certainty, and a last section traces its history in the Reformed churches.
