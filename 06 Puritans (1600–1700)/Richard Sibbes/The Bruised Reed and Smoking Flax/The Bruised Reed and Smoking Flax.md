# The Bruised Reed and Smoking Flax

*Richard Sibbes* — read again from the page images of the 1878 Nisbet edition ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/bruisedreedands00sibbgoog)), a 600 dpi scan in modern spelling. The introductory essay by Alexander Beith that the edition prints in front of the treatise is not included.

Sibbes preaches Matthew 12:20 — *a bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench, till he send forth judgment unto victory* — in twenty-eight short chapters, and the whole of it is one argument: that Christ deals gently with weak and failing faith, and that the same Christ who will not break the reed will carry his own government through to victory. It was the book that turned Richard Baxter, and through Baxter a great deal of what follows it in this section.
