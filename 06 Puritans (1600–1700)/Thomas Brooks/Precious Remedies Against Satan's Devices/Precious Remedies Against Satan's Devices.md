# Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices

*Thomas Brooks* — read again from the page images of Alexander Grosart's edition in *Nichol's Series of Standard Divines*, volume I (Edinburgh, 1866) ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/completeworksoft01broo)), in modern spelling. Grosart set it from the eighth edition of 1676.

Brooks takes 2 Corinthians 2:11 — *lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices* — and answers it by naming the devices one at a time, with the remedies against each: twelve to draw the soul to sin, eight to keep it from holy duties, seven to hold it in a sad and doubting condition, two aimed at the great and the learned, and five more in an appendix to keep sinners from closing with Christ. It is the most practical anatomy of temptation the Puritans wrote.
