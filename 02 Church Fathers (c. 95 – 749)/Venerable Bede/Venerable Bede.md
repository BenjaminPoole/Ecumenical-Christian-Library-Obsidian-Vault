# The Venerable Bede

The Venerable Bede (c. 673–735) was an Anglo-Saxon monk of the twin monastery of
Wearmouth–Jarrow in Northumbria, and the most learned scholar of early medieval
Europe. He wrote in Latin across history, biblical exegesis, hagiography, computus,
grammar, and poetry, and is honoured as a Doctor of the Church.

This collection gathers **Bede's works that exist in good public-domain English
translations**. His masterpiece, the *Ecclesiastical History*, is given complete in
Sellar's revision of Giles; the shorter historical and hagiographical works and the
one public-domain biblical commentary (on Revelation) follow.

> **On completeness.** A truly *complete* Bede in English is not possible from
> public-domain sources. Bede wrote in Latin, and most of his biblical commentaries
> (on Genesis, 1 Samuel, Kings, Ezra–Nehemiah, Proverbs, the Song of Songs, Tobit,
> Habakkuk, Mark, Luke, Acts, the Seven Catholic Epistles, *On the Tabernacle*,
> *On the Temple*), his fifty *Homilies on the Gospels*, and his scientific works
> (*On the Reckoning of Time*, *On the Nature of Things*, *On Times*) were first
> translated into English only in the late twentieth century, in editions that
> remain in copyright (Cistercian Studies; Translated Texts for Historians; Faith
> Wallis). Those works are therefore **not** included here. What follows is the
> complete public-domain English Bede.

## Biblical Commentary

- [[Library/Venerable Bede/Explanation of the Apocalypse/Explanation of the Apocalypse|Explanation of the Apocalypse]] — Bede's commentary on Revelation (c. 710–716), 22 chapters. *(Trans. Edw. Marshall, 1878; uncorrected OCR.)*
