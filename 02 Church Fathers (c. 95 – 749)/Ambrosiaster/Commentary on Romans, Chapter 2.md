# Commentary on Romans, Chapter 2

*While almost all of the works in this vault use Victorian translations, due to the English translations of this commentary being in copyright, it was machine-translated (by Claude, 2026) from the public-domain Latin. It has not been checked by a human translator.*

*Commentary on Paul's Epistle to the Romans, long printed among the works of Ambrose. Its author, whom Erasmus first called "Ambrosiaster", wrote at Rome while Damasus was bishop there (366–384). Chapter 2 of the Epistle; the verse numbers are the Benedictine editors'.*

*(Verse 1.) Therefore thou art inexcusable, O man, whosoever thou art that judgest: for wherein thou judgest another, thou condemnest thyself; for thou that judgest doest the same things.* Since he has shown that he who does evil, and he who consents to those who do it, are worthy of death, he teaches that a man of this kind will be without excuse, lest perhaps one who does it but does not consent to those who do it should think he could excuse himself. For it is not just that this should be allowed him with impunity: because by hypocrisy he shows himself not to be such a man, when he is found to be worse. For he deceives, so that, though he deserves punishment, he appears worthy of honour.

*(Verse 2.) But we are sure that the judgment of God is according to truth against them which commit such things.* That is: we are not ignorant that God will judge these things in truth, since we ourselves judge them. For if they displease us, how much more God, who is more just, and is jealous for His work! When he says, then, that God will judge them in truth, he strikes terror: so that He whom these faithless men call careless, he says will judge the wicked according to truth — that is, will repay most truly according to each one's measure, and will not spare.

*(Verse 3.) And thinkest thou this, O man, that judgest them which do such things, and doest the same, that thou shalt escape the judgment of God?* That is: because the power of judging wicked deeds and debaucheries has been given you to exercise, will you, doing the same things, and with no one to judge you for the present, escape the judgment of God? Certainly not. For if you have escaped God's judgment in this world — since all this power and judgment is from Him — you will not escape it in the time to come. For God will judge in His own person, and with Him flattery and respect of persons have no place. Or at any rate, if it seems just to anyone that such a man should be free from punishment, let him say so. But if it is just that he should not escape, let him believe that God will judge: so that what he judges to be just may be true and may be fulfilled, and he may confess that God, the Maker of the world, looks into the deserts of His work with foresight and care. For if He made it and neglects it, He is called a bad workman: since by neglecting it He shows that the things He made were not made good. But since it cannot be denied that He made them good — for it is unworthy and incredible that a good being should have made evil things — it must be said that He takes care of them: since it will be an insult to Him, and an accusation, if He is brought in as neglectful of the good things He has made; seeing that by His nod and providence life itself is sustained through the service of the elements, as the Lord Himself says, *He maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust* (Matthew 5:45). Does not He who gives this care, then, see to preserving what He made, so as to reward those who love Him, and condemn those who despise Him?

*(Verse 4.) Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and forbearance and longsuffering; not knowing that the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance?* He says this so that a man may not think he has escaped because the goodness of God bears with him while he sins for a long time; nor think that His patience is to be despised, as though He did not care for human affairs. Let him understand rather that he is passed over for this reason, that God's judgment has not been promised for this life; so that in the life to come he will repent of not having believed God to be judge. For, set in punishment, he will repent without the fruit of repentance, because he did not believe the judgment of God which he now sees to be true. For he whom long-suffering patience has not amended must needs be amended more sharply — or rather tormented with everlasting punishments. He speaks most severely, knowing that a malicious mind cannot easily be called back from its vices except by fear.

*(Verses 5–6.) But after thy hardness and impenitent heart treasurest up unto thyself wrath against the day of wrath and revelation of the righteous judgment of God; who will render to every man according to his deeds.* Hoping that his sins will go unpunished, he not only persists, remaining unconverted and intractable, but sins still more bitterly, sure that no vengeance is to come, having an impenitent heart, not knowing that he is gathering wrath for himself in the day of wrath. For he must needs be struck with a fiercer punishment — or rather tormented in everlasting flames — who not only would not amend himself during God's long patience, but, adding contempt besides, heaped up his sins. The day of wrath is for sinners, the day on which they will be punished; and it is wrath to them because they feel the punishment in the day of the revelation of God's righteous judgment. For what is now denied to be coming will be revealed, that is, recognised; and so, when what is believed not to exist is shown, it is revealed. For what is plain to believers is shown to those who deny it, so that even against their will they may confess God's righteous judgment, when He renders to every man according to his works. Will they not confess that this is justly done, when they see the evils that men have done to one another avenged?

*(Verse 7.) To them who by patient continuance in well doing seek for glory and honour and immortality, eternal life.* Since God's judgment has been proclaimed to be just, he has declared what it will be for the good. He means those who, knowing God's patience — that it is drawn out partly for correction, partly for a greater vengeance on those who do not correct themselves — repent of their former works and live well, relying on the confidence of faith, lest, staying long here, they should incur the loss of the life that is promised. He will give them, then, glory and honour. And lest this should seem no great thing compared with the present life — since here too men are seen in glory and honour — he added, *and incorruption*: so that from this another glory and another honour might be understood, when incorruption shall go along with them. For in the present life honour or glory is often lost, because he who gives it is corruptible, and what he gives, and he who receives it. But in the day of God's judgment an incorruptible honour and glory will be given, so that all shall be co-eternal: for the very substance will be glorified, by a kind of change answering to their deserts. They seek eternal life, then, who are good not only in profession but also in life.

*(Verse 8.) But unto them that are contentious, and do not obey the truth, but obey unrighteousness, indignation and wrath, (verse 9) tribulation and anguish* — that is, anguish upon anguish. Distrusting the judgment of God that is to come through Christ, and so despising His patience as well, they strive to make it void, though it is true and stable. And they trust unrighteousness: for it is unrighteousness to deny what God has foretold will come. So he set down three other things fitting for unbelief, saying, *wrath and indignation and anguish*. For the wrath is not in Him who judges, but in him who is judged, as he is found guilty. For God is said to be angry so that He may be believed to be about to punish; for God's nature is free from these passions. And that He may be believed not only to be angry but also to punish, he added, *and indignation*: indignation is what, by adding it to wrath, he means to show that God will avenge the wrong done Him. And anguish is that by which the man bound by the sentence will be afflicted in punishment.

*(Verse 9.) Upon every soul of man that doeth evil.* By "work" here he means not only deeds, but also the profession of faithlessness: for he is speaking of the unbeliever. And so he said *upon the soul*, that you may understand a spiritual punishment, not a bodily one: for the soul will be straitened by invisible punishments. *Of the Jew first, and also of the Gentile.* He always puts the Jew first, by the privilege of the fathers, whether he is to be crowned first or condemned: for the believing Jew is the more honourable because of Abraham, but the unbelieving Jew is to be dealt with worse, because he refused the gift promised to the fathers.

*(Verse 10.) But glory, honour, and peace, to every man that worketh good, to the Jew first, and also to the Gentile.* As he set down three penalties for the unbelieving, so he sets down three glories for the faithful: that they may have a twofold honour, as sons of God; that the glory may answer to the change; and peace, because those who live well will be at rest in the world to come, moved by no disturbance; since every man who keeps himself from what is contrary has peace with the Judge.

*(Verse 11.) For there is no respect of persons with God.* Since he has shown that the Jew or the Greek is not despised by God, provided he believes in Christ, but that the righteousness of faith is reckoned to both alike; and again that unbelievers are guilty alike, because circumcision without faith will do the more harm, and uncircumcision through faith will profit: by this he teaches that God is no respecter of persons. For He does not follow the privilege of birth, so as to receive the unbeliever for the fathers' sake, or cast away the believer because his parents were unworthy; but He either rewards or condemns each one by his own desert.

*(Verse 12.) For as many as have sinned without law shall also perish without law.* How can one sin without law, when we are all alike subject to the natural law? But he is speaking of the law of Moses, to which the Jews are bound while they do not believe; and the Gentiles too, but long ago, because they would not join themselves to it. The unbelieving Gentiles, then, are made guilty in two ways: because they neither assented to the law given through Moses, nor received the grace of Christ; and so it is just that they should perish. As, then, he who sins without the law shall perish, so he who has kept the law without the law shall be justified. For he who keeps righteousness by nature is a guardian of the law. For if *the law is not made for a righteous man* (1 Timothy 1:9), but for the unrighteous, he who does not sin is a friend of the law. Faith alone is wanting to him, by which he may be made perfect: because to keep from what is contrary will profit him nothing with God unless he has received faith in God, so as to be righteous by both: for that is the righteousness of time, this of eternity. *And as many as have sinned in the law shall be judged by the law.* Since the Jews have the law, in which salvation was promised them, if they do not believe, or do not receive what was promised, they will be judged by the law under which they lived. For they will pay the penalty with the law itself accusing them. Indeed, among the Jews themselves the case of the Jews is almost graver than that of the Gentiles: for as, when they believe, they are preferred, so, when they disbelieve, they are found worse. For he who has lost what he had displeases more than he who could not obtain what he did not hope for: the one did not enter the palace; the other was thrown out of it.

*(Verse 13.) For not the hearers of the law are just before God, but the doers of the law shall be justified.* He says this because the just are not those who hear the Law, but those who believe in Christ, whom the same Law promised: and this is to do the Law. Everyone who does not believe the Law does not do the Law, since he does not receive Him to whom the Law bears witness. But he who does not appear to be in the Law, because he is uncircumcised in the flesh, yet believes in Christ — he is said to have done the Law. And he who says he is in the Law, that is, the Jew, becomes a hearer of the Law, not a doer, because he does not believe in the Christ written of in the Law; as Philip said to Nathanael, *We have found him, of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write, Jesus* (John 1:45).

*(Verse 14.) For when the Gentiles, which have not the law, do by nature the things contained in the law.* By Gentiles he means Christians, as he says in another place, *For I speak to you Gentiles* (Romans 11:13): because, being uncircumcised, they keep neither new moons nor the sabbath nor the law of foods, and, with nature for their guide, they believe in God and Christ, that is, in the Father and the Son; for this is to keep the Law — to acknowledge the God of the Law. For this is the first part of wisdom: to fear God the Father, from whom are all things, and the Lord Jesus His Son, through whom are all things. Nature itself, then, by its own judgment, acknowledges its Creator, not through the law but through the reason of nature: for the work discerns in itself the workman.

*(Verse 15.) These, having not the law, are a law unto themselves: which shew the work of the law written in their hearts.* It is the same sense: for while they believe with nature for their guide, they show the work of the law, not by the letter but by conscience. And the work of the law is faith, which, when a man shows it to God along with his words, he shows by a natural judgment that he is a law to himself: because of his own accord he does what the law commands, in believing in Christ. *Their conscience also bearing witness.* They believe with their inward conscience as witness, because they are conscious that what they believe befits them: for it is fitting for a creature to believe in its Maker and to reverence Him, and it is no absurdity that a servant should acknowledge his master.

*(Verse 16.) And their thoughts the mean while accusing or else excusing one another; in the day when God shall judge the secrets of men by Jesus Christ according to my gospel.* He has said that the unbelieving Jews will be judged by the Law — for the Law will accuse them, which promised them Christ, whom they would not receive when He came — but the Gentiles, who certainly have not the Law, will be judged by conscience if they would not believe. First, the believing Gentiles will be the accusers of the unbelieving Gentiles; as the Lord says to the unbelieving Jews of His disciples, *They shall be your judges* (Matthew 12:27): because the faithlessness of the Jews will be judged by the faith of the apostles, who, though they were of the Jews, believed while the Jews disbelieved. Then the Gentiles will be accused by their own thoughts, if, though pricked at heart, they would not believe the faith and power of the Creator. Or if, from a kind of stupor, they do not consider, so as to believe the Lord's words and deeds, they will excuse themselves by their conscience in the day of judgment, because such a one did not think he ought to believe; and he will be judged not as malicious but as ignorant: yet they will not be without punishment, because it is not permitted to be ignorant of this. For he means the Gentiles in two kinds, the believing as well as the unbelieving: above he spoke of the Gentiles who believe, and afterwards added something of those Gentiles who do not believe; so that, as the believers are praised by their own conscience, so the unbelievers may be accused by their own conscience. For though he who has not believed may seem to himself to be little guilty, because he could not work the matter out for himself, yet he is convicted by reason: because he did not persuade himself that this was true, which he had seen confirmed by the testimony of mighty works, and many following it.

I think it can also be understood thus, if he is believed to have spoken of the whole question with regard to the Gentiles who believe; since he says, *For what have I to do to judge them also that are without?* (1 Corinthians 5:12); and, *He that believeth not*, he says, *is condemned already* (John 3:18); and, *The ungodly shall not stand in the judgment* (Psalm 1:5). For those who sin without law shall also perish without law; and so they will not be able to stand before the judgment seat of Christ and plead their cause, because when they rise they will be led to hell. Those, then, whom he says will be accused or excused by their own thoughts in the day of God's judgment are Christians who differ from the catholic truth, thinking otherwise about Christ, or about the meaning of the law in the tradition of the Church: whether Cataphrygians, or Novatians, or Donatists, or the other heretics. The thoughts of these will accuse one another in the day of judgment. If a man understood that the catholic teaching was true, and would not follow it lest he should seem to have been corrected, being ashamed to withdraw from what he had long held, he will be accused by his own thought in the day of judgment. For two thoughts in a man will accuse each other, the good and the bad. The good accuses the bad, because it spoke against the truth; and the bad in turn accuses the good, because it did not follow as it perceived. And by this he is made guilty, who thought, perceiving that the catholic Church was good and true, and yet remained in heresy or schism. But his thoughts will excuse one another who always thought this, judging what he followed to be profitable. For he will say: "In my thoughts I always judged that which I followed to be profitable: this was my faith." His case will be lighter, though it must be corrected, because he will not be accused by his conscience in the day of judgment. In this way will the secrets of men be judged in the day of God's judgment, through Jesus Christ our Lord.

*(Verse 17.) Behold, thou art called a Jew.* The surname of Jew comes from the privilege of their parents, in the same way that they are called Israelites. Yet if we wish to take in all that belongs to the matter, we ought to notice that the surname of Jew has a threefold meaning. First, because they are sons of Abraham, who through the merit of his faith was set as father of all the nations. Next, because of Jacob, who was called Israel for the increase of his faith: for the dignity begun in the father is ennobled in the sons. Thirdly, not so much because of Judah, as because of Christ: because He sprang from Judah according to the flesh, they are surnamed Jews; for what was to be in Christ was signified in Judah. For it was said, *Judah shall be your leader* (Judges 20:18); and, *Judah, thou art he whom thy brethren shall praise* (Genesis 49:8). This praise is not shown to have been given to Judah, but to Christ, whom all those whom He has deigned to call His brethren praise every day. For He Himself said to the women, *Go, and tell my brethren that I go before you into Galilee* (Matthew 28:7, 10). For who would not praise Him by whose kindness he lives? The Apostle, then, wished all to be taken in under this meaning, which is last in order but most perfect in worth: and the Jews, not understanding this, claim for themselves the name of Judah after the flesh.

*(Verse 18.) And restest in the law, and makest thy boast of God, and knowest his will, and approvest the things that are more excellent, being instructed out of the law.* He would not have it seem a very great thing if a Jew believes, since he has been instructed by the Law; but a very perilous one if he does not believe, for he has the Law for his guide. Yet that the believer is put first is the merit of the fathers: for though a man may flourish by himself, he still blushes for his own people.

*(Verses 19–20.) And art confident that thou thyself art a guide of the blind, a light of them which are in darkness, an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of babes, which hast the form of knowledge and of the truth in the law.* These things are true: for this is the teaching of the Law — to instruct the ignorant, and to bring the profane under God; or to call the ungodly away from the worship of idols, by the promise that was made through the Law, to the confidence of a better hope. Rightly, then, does a teacher of the law glory in these things, because he hands on the form of truth. But if he does not receive Him whom the Law promised, he glories in the Law in vain, and does it wrong, while he spurns the Christ promised in the Law. And he will no longer be an instructor of the foolish, nor a teacher of babes, nor a light of them that are in darkness, but a guide of all these to perdition.

*(Verse 21.) Thou therefore which teachest another, teachest thou not thyself?* That is: you who reprove the Gentiles for being without the Law and without God, do not reprove yourself: for, disbelieving in the Christ promised in the Law, you are found in the very things you reprove.

*(Verse 22.) Thou that preachest a man should not steal, dost thou steal?* For you do what you preach ought not to be done: for while by a bad interpretation you steal away faith in Christ, you deny that our Christ was promised in the Law. *Thou that sayest a man should not commit adultery, dost thou commit adultery?* You adulterate the Law, when you take away the truth of Christ and put a lie in its place: which is why he speaks in another place of those who *corrupt the word of God* (2 Corinthians 2:17). *Thou that abhorrest idols, dost thou commit sacrilege?* You are sacrilegious when you deny Christ, whom the Law and the word of the prophets show to be God. For Isaiah says, *Surely God is in thee; and there is none else, there is no God beside thee. Verily thou art a God, and we knew it not, O God of Israel, the Saviour* (Isaiah 45:14–15). Did the Jews say of God the Father, "For thou art God, and we knew it not," when the whole Law proclaims the authority of God the Father, from whom are all things? But because the Son of God always appeared, yet who He was lay hidden, when He is recognised after the resurrection it is said to Him in confession, "For thou art God, and we knew it not." And He who in the Law was thought to be only an Angel, and the captain of the Lord's host — when He is understood to be the Son of God, it is said to Him with thanksgiving, "For thou art God, and we knew it not." By this, then, he signifies that it was He who had indeed appeared to the patriarchs as God, and was afterwards made flesh, but had not been understood by all.

*(Verse 23.) Thou that makest thy boast of the law, through breaking the law dishonourest thou God?* You are a breaker of the Law when you pass over the meaning of the Law, which concerns the incarnation and the Godhead of Christ; and you dishonour God, when you do not receive the witness He gave of His Son. For He said, *This is my beloved Son* (Matthew 3:17).

*(Verse 24.) For the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you, as it is written* (Isaiah 52:5). Isaiah the prophet said this — that God's name is blasphemed among the Gentiles — because they did not perceive that the Jews had been handed over to them for their sins, but gave the glory to their own idols, as though they had overcome the God of the Jews in the Jews. So too in the apostles' time the name of God was blasphemed in Christ: because the Jews, by denying that Christ is God, blasphemed the Father too; as the Lord says, *Whosoever shall receive me, receiveth not me, but him that sent me* (Luke 9:48; Mark 9:37). And so it was blasphemed among the Gentiles, because the Jews strove to persuade the believing Gentiles that Christ was not to be believed to be God; so that the Gentiles' blasphemy set out from the Jews, or from the followers of Photinus.

*(Verse 25.) For circumcision verily profiteth, if thou keep the law.* It can be said in reply: "If circumcision profits, why is it set aside?" But it profits then, if you keep the Law. So circumcision is to be held, and, that it may profit, the Law is to be kept. Why, then, did he forbid what he shows to profit if the Law is kept? It seems wrongly forbidden, a thing which is not displeasing in itself, but is said to be made void through another's neglect. *But if thou be a breaker of the law, thy circumcision is made uncircumcision.* This is what he means: that if the Law has not been kept, the Jew is made a Gentile. But he put circumcision for the race of Abraham, because circumcision is from Abraham: for he could not build up again what he had pulled down. He says this to teach that to be of the race of Abraham profits then, if the Law is kept — that is, if men believe in Christ, who was promised to Abraham: because those justified by faith have both their own merit and are raised up by the honour of the fathers. For all salvation in the Law is concerning Christ. He, then, keeps the Law who believes in Christ; but if he does not believe, he is a transgressor of the Law, because he does not receive the Christ whom the Law sang would come for justification, which the Law itself could not give; and it will profit him nothing to be called a son of Abraham: for he is a son of Abraham by merit who follows the faith through which Abraham was found worthy of God. Hence he says, *Thy circumcision is made uncircumcision*: that is, you have become like a Gentile, not believing in Him who, in the sign of circumcision, was promised to Abraham as his son.

*(Verse 26.) Therefore if the uncircumcision keep the righteousness of the law, shall not his uncircumcision be counted for circumcision?* The righteousness of the Law is the Christian faith, as he says in another place, *Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth* (Romans 10:4). It is plain, then, that if a Gentile believes in Christ, he becomes a son of Abraham, who is the father of faith.

*(Verse 27.) And shall not uncircumcision which is by nature, if it fulfil the law, judge thee, who by the letter and circumcision dost transgress the law?* The Gentile who believes in Christ, with nature for his guide, condemns the Jew, to whom the Law had promised Christ, and who would not believe in Him when He came. As great as the glory is, then, of which the Gentile is worthy, who by nature alone understood the Author — as the apostle Peter says, *And killed the Author of life* (Acts 3:15) — so much the more is the Jew to be punished, who acknowledged Christ as Author neither by nature nor by the Law.

*(Verses 28–29.) For he is not a Jew, which is one outwardly; neither is that circumcision, which is outward in the flesh: but he is a Jew, which is one inwardly; and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of God.* It is not obscure that he denies that circumcision of the flesh has praise with God — for Abraham was not justified because he was circumcised; he was justified because he believed, and was circumcised afterwards — but says that circumcision of the heart is praiseworthy with God. For to circumcise the heart is to acknowledge the Creator, error being cut away. And because the circumcision of the heart was to come, Moses first said, *Circumcise the hardness of your heart* (Deuteronomy 10:16); and Jeremiah likewise, *Take away the foreskins of your heart* (Jeremiah 4:4). He said this to the Jews when they were following idols: for there is a veil about the heart, which he who is turned to God circumcises; because faith takes away the cloud of error, and bestows a perfect knowledge of God in the mystery of the Trinity, which was unknown from the ages. The praise of this circumcision, then, is from God, hidden from men: for it is the merit of the heart, not of the flesh, that God looks upon. But the praise of the Jews is from men: for they glory in the circumcision of the flesh, as coming from the fathers. Hence he says among other things, *Whose glory is in their shame, who mind earthly things* (Philippians 3:19): that is, those who think circumcision of the flesh a glory. For he minds earthly things who glories in the flesh; but he who glories in the spirit, his praise is from God: for one believes with the spirit, not with the flesh.
