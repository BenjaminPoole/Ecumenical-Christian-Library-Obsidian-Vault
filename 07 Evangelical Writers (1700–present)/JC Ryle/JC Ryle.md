# J. C. Ryle

**John Charles Ryle (1816–1900)** was the first Bishop of Liverpool and the outstanding evangelical
Anglican writer of the Victorian era. A Christ Church man and a fine athlete who lost the family fortune
at twenty-five, he spent thirty-eight years in country parishes in Suffolk before Disraeli sent him to
Liverpool at sixty-four. He wrote as he preached — plainly, directly, and for ordinary people.

All of Ryle’s works are in the public domain.

## Sources and honesty about them

- ***Expository Thoughts on the Gospels*** and several other works here are taken from the **Grace Gems**
  edition, whose text is *lightly modernised* (for example “Holy Spirit” for Ryle’s “Holy Ghost”, and
  trimmed Scripture references); Ryle’s original footnotes are not present in that edition. It is,
  however, the only complete free digital text of all four Gospel volumes.
- ***Holiness***, ***The Upper Room*** (CCEL) and ***Practical Religion*** and ***The Cross***
  (Project Gutenberg) are **Ryle’s original text, with his own footnotes**.
- **Not included:** *Knots Untied*, the complete *Old Paths*, and the complete *Light from Old Times*
  exist only as uncorrected OCR scans of printed volumes, and were deliberately left out rather than
  added in poor quality. The *Christian Leaders* chapter on **Augustus Toplady** is likewise missing
  from the available source.

- **Texts that appear twice.** Ryle reworked his own tracts into his books, so a few pieces are held here in two
  forms, under the title each was published with. The main ones: *[[Holiness 17 Thirst Relieved|Thirst Relieved]]*
  (Holiness) and *[[UR 07 Let Any Man Come|Let Any Man Come]]* (The Upper Room); *[[UR 05 The Good Way|The Good Way]]*
  and the tract *[[Ryle - The Old Paths|The Old Paths]]*; *[[Holiness 08 Moses — An Example|Moses — An Example]]* and the
  tract *[[Ryle - Faith’s Choice|Faith’s Choice]]*; *[[UR 19 Thoughts for Young Men|Thoughts for Young Men]]* and the tract
  *[[Ryle - Hints to Young Men|Hints to Young Men]]*. Both are kept.

Each note carries its own `source:` field recording exactly where its text came from.
