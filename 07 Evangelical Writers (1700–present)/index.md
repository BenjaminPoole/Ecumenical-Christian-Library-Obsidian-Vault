# Evangelical Writers

Evangelical preaching and divinity in English, from the eighteenth-century revivals to the early twentieth century. This is the largest section of the library: 6,926 notes across four authors and one collective work, all of them in the public domain.

## Charles Spurgeon

Charles Haddon Spurgeon (1834–1892), the "Prince of Preachers," was the Reformed Baptist pastor of the New Park Street Chapel and then the Metropolitan Tabernacle in London for thirty-eight years. He left one of the largest bodies of published work of any Christian author: the weekly sermons alone run to 63 volumes and 3,512 sermons, preached between 1855 and 1917. Alongside them are *The Treasury of David*, *Morning and Evening*, *Lectures to My Students*, *All of Grace*, *The Soul Winner*, *Faith's Checkbook*, *John Ploughman's Talk*, *Commenting and Commentaries*, *Till He Come*, the *Sermons on Proverbs*, and *A Puritan Catechism*. Texts from the [Christian Classics Ethereal Library](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon) and [The Spurgeon Archive](https://archive.spurgeon.org/treasury/treasury.php).

Author index: **[[Charles Spurgeon|Charles Spurgeon]]** *(4,633 notes)*

## John Wesley

John Wesley (1703–1791), Anglican cleric, evangelist, and — with his brother Charles — founder of Methodism. The collection gathers his *Sermons on Several Occasions* (all 141, in the Jackson 1872 numbering) and the *Explanatory Notes* on the whole Bible, which together are the doctrinal standards of Methodism, along with the *Journal*, the prose treatises and appeals, and the *Poetical Works*.

Author index: **[[John Wesley|John Wesley]]** *(1,399 notes)*

## Jonathan Edwards

Jonathan Edwards (1703–1758), pastor at Northampton, missionary at Stockbridge, and the foremost theologian of the Great Awakening. The two-volume *Works* in the Banner of Truth edition — revised and corrected by Edward Hickman, 1834 — from the [CCEL](https://ccel.org/ccel/edwards) text: Volume 1 opens with Sereno E. Dwight's *Memoirs* and the major treatises, Volume 2 holds the sermons, the shorter treatises, the *Life and Diary of David Brainerd*, and the biblical notes.

Author index: **[[je-index|The Works of Jonathan Edwards]]** *(589 notes)*

## J. C. Ryle

John Charles Ryle (1816–1900), first Bishop of Liverpool and the outstanding evangelical Anglican writer of the Victorian era. He spent thirty-eight years in country parishes in Suffolk before Disraeli sent him to Liverpool at sixty-four, and he wrote as he preached — plainly, directly, and for ordinary people. *Holiness*, the *Expository Thoughts on the Gospels*, *Practical Religion*, *Christian Leaders of the Last Century*, *Coming Events and Present Duties*, *The Upper Room*, and the shorter tracts.

Author index: **[[JC Ryle|J. C. Ryle]]** *(214 notes)*

## The Fundamentals

Ninety essays published between 1910 and 1915 by the Testimony Publishing Company of Chicago, funded by the brothers Lyman and Milton Stewart and edited in turn by A. C. Dixon, Louis Meyer, and R. A. Torrey. Written by leading conservative theologians of Britain and North America in defence of orthodox Protestant belief against the higher criticism and Darwinism, and mailed free of charge to pastors and missionaries across the English-speaking world. The work gave its name to the movement later called *fundamentalism*. Given here in the four-volume arrangement edited by Torrey for the Bible Institute of Los Angeles (1917).
