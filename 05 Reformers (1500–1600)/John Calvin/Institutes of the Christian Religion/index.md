# Institutes of the Christian Religion

The *Institutes of the Christian Religion* is John Calvin's systematic summary of Protestant doctrine and the single most influential theological work of the Reformation. First published in Latin in 1536 as a short six-chapter catechism, it grew through successive revisions until the definitive 1559 Latin edition, in which Calvin arranged eighty chapters into four books following the order of the Apostles' Creed: God the Creator, God the Redeemer in Christ, the mode of obtaining Christ's grace, and the external means or aids (the Church and sacraments).

This text is Henry Beveridge's 1845 English translation, made for the Calvin Translation Society, taken from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library's ThML edition (ccel.org). The famous Prefatory Address to Francis I and Calvin's own prefatory pieces are included as unnumbered notes before Book I, and each book's Argument precedes its chapters; the One Hundred Aphorisms summarizing the work follow Book IV. CCEL's scripture-reference tables, word indexes, and the modern editorial introduction are omitted. Footnotes are renumbered per note.
