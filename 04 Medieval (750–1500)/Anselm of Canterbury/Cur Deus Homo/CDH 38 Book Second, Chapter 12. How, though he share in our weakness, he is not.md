# Book Second, Chapter 12. How, though he share in our weakness, he is not therefore miserable

*Boso.* All these things plainly show that he ought to be mortal and to partake of our weaknesses. But all these things are our miseries. Will he then be miserable?

*Anselm.* No, indeed! For as no advantage which one has apart from his choice constitutes happiness,
so there is no misery in choosing to bear a loss, when the choice is a wise one and made without compulsion.

*Boso.* Certainly, this must be allowed.
