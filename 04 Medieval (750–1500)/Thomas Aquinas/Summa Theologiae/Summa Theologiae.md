# Summa Theologiae

The *Summa Theologiae* (or *Summa Theologica*) of St. Thomas Aquinas (c. 1225–1274), written 1265–1274 and left unfinished at his death. Translated by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province (Second and Revised Edition, 1920); public domain. Text from [New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/).

The work is arranged in Parts, each divided into Questions, each Question into Articles. Every Article poses a question, states Objections, gives the *sed contra* ("On the contrary"), Aquinas’s own answer ("I answer that"), and Replies to the Objections.

## Parts

- **[[Prima Pars|Prima Pars]]** — 119 questions. Sacred doctrine, the One God, the Trinity, creation, the angels, man, divine government.
- **[[Prima Secundae|Prima Secundæ]]** — 114 questions. Man’s last end, human acts, passions, habits, virtues and vices, law, grace.
- **[[Secunda Secundae|Secunda Secundæ]]** — 189 questions. The theological and cardinal virtues, and states of life, treated in detail.
- **[[Tertia Pars|Tertia Pars]]** — 90 questions. The Incarnation, the life of Christ, and the sacraments (Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance).
- **[[Supplement|Supplementum]]** — 99 questions. Completed from Aquinas’s earlier writings: Penance (continued), the Resurrection, and the last things.
