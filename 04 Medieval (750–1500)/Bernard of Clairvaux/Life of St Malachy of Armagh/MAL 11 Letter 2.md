# Letter 2

To Malachy. 1141 or 1142.[^1]

(Epistle 356.)

To Malachy, by the grace of God bishop, legate of the Apostolic See,
Brother Bernard, called to be abbot of Clairvaux, if the prayer of a
sinner can do anything, and if the devotion of a poor man is of any
advantage.

We have done what your holiness commanded, not perhaps as it was worthy
to be done, yet as well as was possible considering the time in which we
live. So great evil everywhere struts about among us that it was
scarcely possible to do the little that has been done. We have sent only
a few grains of seed,[^2] as you see, to sow at least a small part of
that field into which the true Isaac once went out to meditate,
when Rebekah was first brought to him by Abraham's servant, to be
happily joined to him in everlasting marriage.[^3] And the seed is not
to be despised concerning which we find that word fulfilled at this time
in your regions,[^4] Except the Lord of Sabaoth had left us a seed, we
had been as Sodoma, and been made like unto Gomorrha.[^5] I,
therefore, have sown, do you water, and God shall give the
increase.[^6] All the saints who are with you we salute through you,
humbly commending ourselves to their holy prayers and yours. Farewell.

[^1]: 

[^2]: , § 39).

[^3]: Gen. xxiv. 63 ff.—Cp. ii. 13, where the same passage of Genesis is referred to. It is there (§ 12) explained that the field is the world, which has been placed in charge of the Pope.

[^4]: . I read .

[^5]: Rom. ix. 29 (inexact quotation).

[^6]: 1 Cor. iii. 6 .
