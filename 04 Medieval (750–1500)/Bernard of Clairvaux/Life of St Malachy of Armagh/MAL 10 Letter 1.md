# Letter 1

To Malachy. 1141.[^1]

(Epistle 341.)

To the venerable lord and most blessed father, Malachy, by the grace of
God archbishop of the Irish, legate of the Apostolic See, Brother
Bernard called to be abbot of Clairvaux, [desiring] to find grace with
the Lord.

1. Amid the manifold anxieties and cares of my heart,[^2] by the
multitude of which my soul is sore vexed,[^3] the brothers coming
from a far country[^4] that they may serve the Lord,[^5] thy
letter, and thy staff, they comfort me:[^6] the letter, as a proof of
good will; the staff, to support my weak body; the brothers, because
they serve the Lord in a humble spirit.[^7] We have received them
all, we are pleased with all, all alike work together for good.[^8]
But as to the wish that you have expressed that two of the
brothers[^9] should be sent to look out a place for you beforehand,
having taken counsel with the brothers, we have not thought it meet that
they should be separated one from another[^10] until Christ be more
fully formed in them,[^11] until they are wholly instructed in the
battles of the Lord.[^12] When therefore they have been taught in the
school of the Holy Spirit, when they have been endued with power from
on high,[^13] then at length the sons shall return to their father that
they may sing the Lord's song, not now in a strange land,[^14] but
in their own.

2. But do you yourselves in the mean time, according to the wisdom
given you[^15] by the Lord, look out beforehand and prepare
beforehand a place for them,[^16] like the places which you have seen
here, apart from the commotions of the world. For the time is at
hand[^17] when, by the operation of the grace of God, we shall bring
forth for you new men out of the old.[^18] Blessed be the Name of
the Lord for ever,[^19] of whose only gift it cometh that[^20] I have
sons in common with you, whom your preaching planted and our
exhortation watered, but God gave the increase.[^21] We beseech your
holiness to preach the word of the Lord[^22] so that you may give
knowledge of salvation unto His people.[^23] For a double necessity
is laid upon you,[^24] both from your office as legate and your duty as
bishop. Finally, since in many things we offend all,[^25] and, being
often thrown among the men of this age, we are much besmirched with the
dust of the world, I commend myself to your prayers and to those of your
companions, that in His fountain of mercy Jesus Christ, himself the
fountain of pity, may deign to wash and cleanse us, who said to Peter,
If I wash thee not, thou shalt have no part with me.[^26] And, indeed,
I not only earnestly entreat this of you, but also require it as in some
sense the payment of a debt, since I cry to the Lord for you, if the
prayer of a sinner can do anything. Farewell in the Lord.
